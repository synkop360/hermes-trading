# Volume-conditioned reversal: research prerequisite

September 30, 2026. Status: data feasibility only; no strategy returns, training, parameter search or orders. Account constraints remain USD50, France, unchanged default risk caps. This candidate is related to the broad reversal family already investigated, so a favorable result would not erase the project's multiple-testing history.

## Economic question

Can unusually quiet trading distinguish temporary price pressure from information-driven price changes, making a daily cross-asset reversal profitable after executable prices, funding, turnover and small-order constraints? This differs from the rejected funding-crowding signal: the proposed conditioning variable is each asset's volume relative to its own history, not funding extremes. It remains a hypothesis, not a recommendation.

[Bianchi, Babiak and Dickerson (2022), Riksbank working paper 413](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/rapporter/working-papers/2022/no.-413-trading-volume-and-liquidity-provision-in-cryptocurreny-markets.pdf) motivates investigating daily reversal conditional on standardized volume deviations from a 30-day trend. Its double sort first groups prior-day returns, then volume within return groups. Stronger results concern smaller, less liquid assets; large liquid assets are less encouraging. Closing-price execution is hypothetical. The text's 30/40 basis-point long/short cost assumption differs from Table 5's 20/30 caption; do not import its reported net performance or presume those costs match Kraken. The paper's wording on standardization also requires clarification before any claimed replication.

[Farag et al. (2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378426625000317) provides separate motivation through links between reversal compensation, risk and liquidity. Its abstract does not establish a deployable small-account rule. No headline academic return is treated as an expected account return.

No cross-asset dollar-volume ranking is justified until units are confirmed.

## Gates before an economic experiment

1. Reconstruct a historical universe including retired markets; determine opening/delisting handling and symbol changes. Never select an old backtest universe using today's seven-market liquidity shortlist. A remaining catalog-coverage gap must be disclosed rather than described as survivor-free.
2. Establish volume units and timestamp semantics, with broader checks across listing ages and retired contracts. Missing bars, zero-volume stale prices and delisting outcomes need explicit treatment; do not discard adverse held-period observations.
3. Obtain same-venue funding and historical cost/quote coverage for the proposed evaluation. Existing multi-year funding limitations remain unresolved; prices alone cannot validate net perpetual returns. Current regional minimum quantities cannot silently substitute for historical constraints.
4. Freeze one exact rule, tie treatment, eligible-universe threshold, execution delay, holding period, gross exposure, rounding, fee/slippage assumptions and rejection criteria before calculating returns. Clarify the volume standardization formula or explicitly label a fixed adaptation instead of a replication. A two-position adaptation for USD50 differs substantially from diversified academic portfolios.
5. Use chronological evaluation, cost stress, uncertainty measured across time and actual account-sized feasibility. Prior project years have already been inspected: retrospective checks are not untouched validation. A passing historical screen would still require independent review and prospective paper observation.

Next action: a bounded historical-universe/volume-unit audit, then decide whether the necessary funding and execution evidence exists. Do not run a profitability backtest yet or change the live strategy.

## October 8 continuation: delisting and funding failure modes

The preceding September30 work was progress: it created new catalog and candle evidence. Subsequent saved delisting-source comparison and new October8 endpoint probes now establish material reasons not to proceed directly to a profitability backtest.

The captured September22 version of [Kraken's delisting table](https://support.kraken.com/articles/7526425887508-derivatives-contracts-trading-suspensions-delistings) contains222 distinct PF symbols. Exact matches cover217 of232 expired catalog entries;15 lack exact matches. Four table symbols occur in the then-current catalog, including resumed contracts. PF_UXLINK versus PF_UXLINKUSD is an unresolved naming mismatch, not silently normalized. The RIVER resumption footnote predates its listed settlement date, requiring clarification. Thus a single opening date plus terminal expired flag is insufficient: listing episodes and settlement events must be represented separately. The October8 web check finds the page updated October2 with additional October1 settlements; the saved September counts remain historical, not a current-universe claim.

EOS provides a concrete failure case. The official table gives May30,2025 settlement at USD0.702022 after the15–16 London observation window (14–15UTC). The fetched hourly trade series for May29–June3 returns121 candles, including82 at or after15:00UTC May30. All82 have zero volume and repeat0.7202, approximately2.5894% above settlement. The last nonzero-volume candle is14:00UTC May30. Repeated labels after closure are not evidence of an active market or obtainable exit. This also demonstrates why the earlier contiguous-candle checks alone cannot establish tradability.

The previously saved September30 DOGE funding response has8,908 observations, September24,2025 08:00 through September30,2026 18:00UTC, no duplicate timestamps and seven internal missing hours. EOS's saved history response contains only one rate at settlement. A separate October8 analytics query for May29–31,2025 returns empty timestamp/rate arrays, no errors and no continuation. One terminal rate does not support a funding path; zero internal gaps for a singleton is not full coverage. Missing funding must not be treated as zero or approximated by a surviving asset's rates.

Artifacts: `delistings.json`, `delisting_comparison.json`, rendered source excerpts and hash manifest; `funding_coverage_probe.json`; `EOS_funding_20250529.json`, `EOS_delisting_candles.json`, `delisting_probe_manifest.json`, `delisting_validation.json`. New responses are separately preserved, not substituted for the earlier samples. These are provider-data consistency checks, not independent market-data verification.

Decision: the broad Kraken retrospective net-return experiment remains unsupported by the currently recovered data. Do not remove retired markets merely because their funding is missing, carry stale candle prices through settlement, or present a survivor-only backtest as confirmation. Next assess whether a bounded alternative data source can supply delisted-contract funding and listing episodes; if unavailable, preregister prospective observation as a data-collection stage only. No candidate has qualified for paper trading and no live configuration changed.

## Alternative-provider probe, October 8

[Tardis Kraken Futures documentation](https://docs.tardis.dev/historical-data-details/cryptofacilities.md) and its public exchange metadata identify EOS records from March22,2022 through May31,2025. A free May1,2025 derivative-ticker sample was downloaded successfully:84,626 records, all with current/predicted funding fields and funding timestamps. Raw compressed file, SHA256 manifest, schema summary and timestamp validation are saved under the existing artifact directory (`tardis_*`). This is independently captured exchange-feed evidence that old EOS funding information exists, not proof of complete funding-payment reconstruction. Vendor availability dates are capture coverage, not exact trading eligibility or settlement times.

The provider grants unauthenticated CSV access for the first day of each month. Continuous history requires subscription access under its [billing terms](https://docs.tardis.dev/faq/billing-and-subscriptions.md). A daily strategy cannot be validated from those isolated days; do not change the strategy to trade sample days merely to fit free access. No subscription, account, trial, payment or vendor message was created. Purchasing data remains unjustified for an unproven USD50 strategy.

The updated official Kraken documentation now names `/derivatives/api/v3/historical-funding-rates`; a direct EOS probe succeeds but still supplies only the one terminal rate. It does not solve the older-history gap. The official archive page still advertises the export; prior download403 evidence remains, with no repeated download or access-control workaround this turn.

Consequence: a recoverable historical source has been identified, but continuous free access has not. Before constructing a prospective collector, specify its fixed signal and observation protocol, retention and stopping criteria; collecting observations must not be presented as launching a validated strategy. Existing paper-readiness gates remain unmet. An accessible official historical archive would allow resuming the retrospective route without buying data.
