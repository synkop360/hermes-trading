# Prospective volume-conditioned reversal: fixed observation design

Status: protocol v1, October8,2026, before prospective outcomes. This is preparation for an unvalidated research observation, not permission to send orders or a claim of paper readiness. The user's autonomous research authorization covers free local implementation. No paid services, credentials, account changes or real orders.

## Hypothesis and scope

Temporary cross-asset price pressure is more likely to reverse when recent trading volume is unusually low relative to each asset's own history. Test one concentrated daily long/short adaptation. It is neither a replication of the published diversified portfolio nor independent of earlier reversal-family research. Account is USD50 in France. Preserve current default per-trade risk0.25%, per-symbol gross5%, total gross10%, daily loss0.5%, maximum drawdown3%. Do not increase these limits to obtain trades.

Prior September data audit and October8 checks support availability, not profitability. October8 snapshot has12 crypto contracts passing the established spread<=5bps,24h quote volume>=USD1million, each top side>=USD25 and buffered minimum notional<=USD2.50 screen. Current regional quantities were checked for all16 liquidity-qualified contracts;12 fit. Source responses and rendered specifications are in `cross-sectional-feasibility-v1/oct8_*`. This list is not fixed membership: future decisions must use their own contemporaneously captured eligibility. A single snapshot does not prove persistent liquidity or feasible stop sizing.

## Signal, frozen before outcomes

Decision once daily at00:05UTC. Use only daily trade bars completed by00:00UTC and received before the decision. Require32 contiguous daily bars with positive OHLC and strictly positive volume, verified bucket timing and no known listing discontinuity. Require a confirmed volume-unit interpretation; use consistent base volume within each instrument, not cross-asset raw volume. A constant unit scaling cancels in the log-volume measure; redenominations or changes in contract units invalidate the window.

Let day d be the just-completed day. Prior return is close[d]/close[d-1]-1. Let z=(log(volume[d])-mean(log(volume[d-30:d])))/sample_std(log(volume[d-30:d])), where the slice includes exactly the30 days before d. Reject zero standard deviation. This explicit log-volume standardization is an adaptation, not the ambiguous published formula. No alternate lookback or parameter search.

Universe: currently active, non-post-only USD linear crypto perpetuals with verified EEA minimum quantities, no known suspension, no announced closure within the next48hours, full signal window, and current liquidity/size checks above. Exclude tokenized equities, commodity/FX exposures and unclassified instruments pending classification. No inference of account eligibility from public listing alone. At least9 eligible instruments required. Sort prior returns ascending, ties by symbol. Let k=floor(N/3); losers=first k, winners=last k. In each group choose the instrument with smallest z, ties by symbol, only if z<0. Require selected loser return<0 and selected winner return>0. Otherwise record no signal. Long loser, short winner. Never reverse sides based on performance.

## Observation and modeled execution

Preflight must pass before setting a start date. Start at the first full UTC day after implementation review and data checks; never backdate the prospective period. Observe180 consecutive calendar days split into two fixed90-day blocks. Pre-start candles may warm indicators but no pre-start returns count as prospective evidence.

Capture all public tickers at least once per minute, with UTC request start/finish, server time, raw response and immutable hash. Capture instruments/specification changes and delisting notices; derive eligibility using information actually available at each decision. Public historical downloads may fill pre-start warmup only. Recovery of missed prospective observations must be labeled retrospective and cannot restore a missed execution quote.

At01:00UTC close any prior positions before considering a new pair. Use first valid quote received after01:00:05 and before01:01:05, requiring fresh server time within10seconds of local receipt, positive uncrossed prices and adequate displayed size. Long entry at ask, short at bid; exits on opposite sides. Apply additional adverse slippage10bps per fill,30bps in stress, plus5bps taker commission per fill. Update this protocol before launch if verified actual fees are higher; never change costs after outcomes to obtain a pass. Existing account-specific fees remain unknown, so no account-readiness claim.

Use14-day simple ATR from completed daily true ranges; initial stop distance2*ATR. Size each leg using the minimum of5% current modeled equity and0.25% equity divided by the fractional stop distance plus stressed round-trip fee/slippage and contemporaneous spread reserve. Round quantities down to verified contract increments. Minimum quantity and lot step are different fields and must both be verified. If either leg fails minimum or aggregate gross10% cap, skip the whole pair; no enlargement to minimum. Initially50USD cash; derivatives notional is exposure, not a cash asset purchase. Charge actual modeled fees/funding to equity.

Stops evaluated using executable liquidation-side quotes at each captured minute. After a breach, model exit on the next valid captured quote with adverse slippage; do not assume execution at the stop price. Close the other leg using the same delayed convention. This is a discrete observation model, not a claim about tick-level execution or guaranteed loss bounds. Gaps while holding make the episode unresolved; never discard it as a losing observation. Daily0.5% loss triggers closure and no reentry that UTC day. Peak liquidation-equity drawdown3% stops the experiment's trading model permanently; observation may continue for diagnostic purposes.

Accrue funding from actual rate paths and exact quantity/time exposure using verified Kraken interval semantics. Rates must be obtained for both legs through exit, including delisting. Missing rates, quotes, contract episodes or official settlement outcomes remain unresolved; no zero fill or survivor-only exclusion. Delisted positions use verified settlement rules, fees and prices. Stale candles after delisting are not exits. Modeled margin requirements use EEA rules and liquidation-equity accounting; reject operational readiness if eligibility, collateral or margin behavior remains uncertain.

## Implementation gates still open

### Review clarifications, fixed before launch

Independent review confirmed causal signal construction and the16/12capacity counts but identified execution/accounting ambiguities. These definitions supersede any less-specific language above. The initial protocol is retained as `prospective_protocol_v1_before_review.md`; both versions remain pre-outcome.

**Common base/stress path:** maintain separate cash/equity ledgers but identical selected instruments, quantities and execution timestamps. Size from the smaller of the two current liquidation equities using stress-cost reserves. Use the same raw crossing quotes, adding the respective10/30bps adverse slippage and5bps fees. Any risk halt in either ledger closes both models and applies the same future-entry restriction. This makes stress a conservative paired counterfactual, not an independently retuned strategy.

**Risk definitions:**0.25% applies to each leg, so combined planned stop risk is at most0.5% before unbounded gaps. Long stop=raw entry ask minus2ATR; short stop=raw entry bid plus2ATR. These shared anchors exclude synthetic slippage; quantities include it in the stressed reserve. With crossing reference P, the sizing denominator is2ATR/P + 2*(0.003+0.0005) + (ask-bid)/midpoint. This reserve is a sizing convention, not a guarantee of realized cost or loss. At every valid quote, liquidation equity=cash + directional unrealized price P&L - accrued unpaid funding - estimated adverse-slippage/commission close costs; paid funding is already in cash and is not subtracted twice. Each ledger tracks its own peak starting at50. Drawdown=(peak-equity)/peak. Daily loss=(equity at00:00UTC minus current equity)/equity at00:00UTC, including unrealized P&L, funding and estimated close costs. Missing00:00equity blocks that day's entry. Neither stop nor risk cap assumes fills at a trigger price.

**Pair and closure mechanics:** both legs require valid quotes captured within2seconds of each other; quantities and liquidity are rechecked at entry. If either entry leg is invalid, simulate neither leg and record a skipped pair. This simultaneous-entry abstraction is for research only, not a live atomic-fill claim; paper preparation needs a separate partial-fill/hedge-failure model. Prior holdings close before any new entry; unresolved closure forbids new exposure. Exit each leg on its first valid quote after the scheduled exit/trigger delay; retain differing timestamps and accrue funding per leg. If closure cannot be observed within60seconds, mark the episode unresolved and block passing. Do not synthesize its exit or remove it from the ledger. Stop triggers use first observed executable-side breach, then the next captured minute quote; daily-loss/drawdown closures use the same convention.

**Time boundaries and statistics:** mark daily liquidation equity at00:00UTC using the first valid quote received in[00:00,00:01), with actual quote time recorded; require a usable mark for every scored day. Daily simple returns connect successive marks; both90-day block P&Ls use these boundary equity differences. Pair counts belong to their entry-date block, regardless of exit date. At the day90boundary force both models to close at the first valid synchronized quote after00:00:05 and before00:01:05; carry resulting cash into block2, with exit costs assigned to block1. Reopen only at that day's normal01:00entry, using the frozen rule. On day180end liquidate similarly and include all terminal costs in block2; forbid later entries. Boundary liquidation cash replaces that boundary's ordinary equity mark, making the accounting additive.
